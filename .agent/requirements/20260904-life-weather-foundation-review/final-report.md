# Final Report: TSUZUNE LIFE Weather Foundation Review

## Outcome

The review is complete and ready for the owner's decision. The recommended next move is one isolated, five-working-day Living Archive Field proof. Product source, dependencies, production installation, and Git delivery remain unchanged and unauthorized. The final production-TSUZUNE writeback is pending because the registered MCP runtime rejected writes as stale; no Vault write was applied.

The root diagnosis is that the artistic ceiling is primarily the representation, temporal grammar, camera/world contract, and viewing surface—not TypeScript or WebGL2. The current prototype is visibly alive, but its first reading remains sparse white points on black and its event-following camera does not observe one persistent world.

## Accepted Results

- Preserve every note as one logical, traceable contributor, but retire the requirement that every note remain one visible dot in every frame.
- Require a deterministic contributor receipt before rendering; source-free carrier material may texture or transport a source-selected event but may not invent its cue, region, lifetime, or relationship.
- Keep one persistent world-coordinate representation and an independently integrated camera. Cue changes affect forces/envelopes only; they do not rebase the world or replace camera/feature identities.
- Replace the global 90-second loop with overlapping archive-climate, encounter, and micro-life clocks, entered at a deterministic already-running archive time.
- Use the existing Electron BrowserWindow client area as the eventual immersive surface. The workspace state is lifecycle only; all tool chrome is hidden during viewing and the prior workspace/focus is restored on return.
- Retain TypeScript for Vault observation, score, and provenance. Use direct WebGL2/GLSL for the first isolated proof, Canvas 2D for the static/failure reference, and hold WebGPU for a same-score measured A/B.

## Rejected Results

- Continuing `one note = one always-visible point`, fixed event slots, event-target camera replacement, or screen-space history as the artwork foundation.
- Treating better easing, denser fog, bloom, audio, or more particles as a substitute for persistent world/time/causality.
- Immediate migration to WebGPU, Three.js, Rust/WASM, a native engine, a sidecar process, or a second window without measured artistic or performance need.
- Persistent captions, note-name hover, a visible tab bar, or editor chrome on the viewing surface.
- Any claim that the field, flow, particles, depth layers, or world model are `存在相そのもの`, note value, or true archive relationships.

## Conflicts Resolved

- ART-01 retained `one note = one light` as a conservative identity rule. The parent accepted its non-duplication concern but rejected permanent visibility; identity now lives in the deterministic contributor ledger.
- RENDER-01's initial WebGPU unknown was superseded by the later hidden-Electron capability receipt. Adapter/device/replacement-device creation passed, while performance, visual superiority, recovery in production, and packaged parity remain unproved.
- RENDER-01's old 90-second backend comparison is retained only as a capability/pipeline idea. Adoption now requires the same Living Archive Field snapshot, seed, score, canvas/DPR, and perceptual protocol.
- INTEGRATION-01 allowed a visible tab bar as an early escape route. The final presentation contract chooses hidden chrome in the same BrowserWindow client area, with `Escape` and a focus-revealed return control.
- VERIFY-01 returned `approve-with-corrections`, no P0. Its five P1 corrections—causal receipt, no hidden rebase, pre-registered acceptance, same-score WebGPU gate, and one presentation contract—are integrated into the foundation and vertical slice.
